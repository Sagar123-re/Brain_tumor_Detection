# Brain_tumor_Detection
Brain Tumor Detection System Using a Microstrip Patch Antenna

The project involves designing and simulating a microwave-based brain tumor detection system using a rectangular microstrip patch antenna in CST Studio Suite (CST Microwave Studio). A six-layer human head phantom consisting of skin, fat, skull, dura mater, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and brain tissue was developed, with each layer assigned appropriate dielectric properties to accurately simulate the propagation of electromagnetic waves through the human head. The antenna was positioned close to the head phantom to transmit microwave signals, and the interaction of these signals with different tissue layers was analyzed using full-wave electromagnetic simulations.

The detection mechanism is based on the difference in the dielectric properties of healthy and cancerous brain tissues. Since tumor tissues generally have higher water content, they exhibit higher permittivity and electrical conductivity than normal tissues. These variations alter the reflection, transmission, and absorption characteristics of microwave signals. By analyzing the changes in the reflected electromagnetic waves, the system can identify the possible presence of abnormal tissue, demonstrating the feasibility of microwave imaging for non-invasive brain tumor detection.

The antenna design was optimized to achieve efficient radiation and impedance matching, while its performance was evaluated using key electromagnetic parameters such as Return Loss (S11), Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR), Gain, Radiation Pattern, Electric Field (E-Field), Magnetic Field (H-Field), Surface Current Distribution, and Specific Absorption Rate (SAR). These parameters were used to assess antenna efficiency, signal penetration, radiation characteristics, energy absorption, and overall suitability for biomedical imaging applications.

The project demonstrates the application of microwave engineering, antenna design, electromagnetic simulation, and biomedical engineering in developing a low-cost, portable, and non-invasive brain tumor detection system. Through extensive simulations in CST Studio Suite, the project validates the potential of microwave imaging as an alternative approach for early brain tumor detection while providing practical experience in antenna optimization, RF analysis, and biomedical electromagnetic modeling.
